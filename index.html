How it works. The benchmark is the equal-weight S&P 500 (RSP), which tracks the typical member rather than the few largest companies, so beating it means beating the average stock. Every number is calculated from daily split- and dividend-adjusted closes. Relative performance is the stock's return divided by the index's return over the same window ((1+stock)/(1+index) − 1). RS 12M weighted follows the IBD convention: 40% weight on the latest quarter and 20% on each of the three before it. RS rating is that weighted score's percentile among all S&P 500 members (1–99); the index's own rating is shown in the summary, and only stocks above it are listed. The RS line is stock price ÷ index price; a rising line means the stock is outperforming. Vol vs 50d is the latest day's volume divided by the average of the 50 days before it; 1.5× or more is bold, green on up days and red on down days. Chart setups (VCP, cup with handle, cup with handle at the 50-day, flat base, high tight flag, 3-weeks-tight, pocket pivot) are scanned nightly; the Strategies tab lists them with the exact rules. Near MAs keeps stocks closing at or just above all three of the 21-day EMA, 50-day and 200-day averages, by no more than the % you pick. Data: point-in-time S&P 500 dataset (github.com/Johnbrick123/sp500-data, built from Yahoo Finance and cross-checked against Nasdaq), sector labels from the GICS list. This is a screen, not investment advice.
